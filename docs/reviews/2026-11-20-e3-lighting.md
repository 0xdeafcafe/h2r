# E3 lighting review: the design against the 2003 system and against FrameScene

Review of 2026-11-20. Frozen: later work is in
[e3-lighting.md](../graphics/e3-lighting.md) and the code.

Status: independent review, 2026-11-20, of e3-lighting.md as it stood at
`00dd7430f`. Nothing was built or run for it. The evidence is the repo's
docs and code named in each finding, the public record collected in
[pstencil-history.md](../graphics/pstencil-history.md), and one public
repository read as reference on the day (Sev's shadow shaders, recorded in
[notes/e3-lighting.md](../graphics/notes/e3-lighting.md)). No leaked build,
source or archive was opened. No game data is on this page.

Labels: **Code** read in this repository at the path given. **P**, **P\***,
**C** as in e3-lighting.md (a developer's statement, the same through a
secondary quotation, a community finding). **Estimate** arithmetic to be
checked by a measurement.

## The short version

1. **The technique decision is right, and for more reasons than the page
   gave.** Shadow maps, not stencil volumes. FrameScene adds three reasons
   the page could not have had (section 3).
2. **The per-pixel lighting plan is wrong.** It designs a host-side normal
   buffer and a search for each material's normal map. Retail materials
   already carry per-light, per-pixel, bump-mapped diffuse and specular
   passes, and the native renderer already draws them. The cut renderer's
   lighting half shipped; its shadow half and its content did not.
3. **Everything that is built sits on the path that is going away.** The
   Lighting row lives in `d3d::scaled`, which FrameScene slice 6 takes out
   of game views and slice 10 deletes. With E3 on, the native shadow stage
   stands down today, so the row currently works against "one rendering
   path".
4. **The observer design is superseded.** `FrameScene` is the observer.
5. **Missing altogether:** a budget, a light-count policy, the flashlight,
   weapon and plasma lights, 60 and 120 fps, split screen, and how the hard
   edge is made when a map is coarser than the screen.

The page's headline, "there is nothing of the cut renderer to switch back
on in the retail game", is half right. No volume path survives (the
stencil census stands). But the light accumulation survives whole: every
lit material has a layer-12 diffuse pass that samples its base map, a
normalization cube and its bump map under a projected or spherical light,
and a layer-13 tangent-space specular pass
(`crates/rewrites/native-render/src/light_look.rs`,
[native-lights.md](../graphics/native-lights.md) "Implementations";
10,137 resolved lists on each of those layers against 16,622 on the
lightmap layer, e3-lighting.md "Pass taxonomy: the layers"). pstencil.md
already says the `lightaccum_*` names are passes retail ships. The design
never drew the conclusion.

## 3. The technique, challenged

pstencil.md's four reasons for maps hold (measured cost, open meshes, the
look, the authors' own record). The case for volumes was tried again with
the renderer as it will be:

- **Blended frames.** At 60 and 120 fps the renderer draws poses blended
  between two scenes (framescene.md, section 7). A shadow map is the caster
  drawn again with the vertex program it already has. A volume needs a
  silhouette per light per caster per display frame from skinned
  positions: on the CPU of the render thread, or with an edge mesh that
  skins four positions per vertex. wgpu has no geometry stage.
- **The stencil buffer is taken.** Retail marks the scene's stencil with
  modes that write 0 or `0xff` over whole draws ("Stencil: every use,
  named"), and typed parts carry those marks (`PartFlags`). Counting
  volumes need the buffer to themselves, so a second depth-stencil at the
  display scale and a depth copy.
- **The content has the passes for maps and none for volumes.** Layers 10
  and 11 have about ten thousand resolved lists each; alpha-killed casters
  punch holes in a map through their own material and would cast solid as
  volumes.
- **The sun is not a light in retail data.** It is baked. Volumes would
  still end in the same multiply over baked light that a map ends in.

What volumes keep: an exact edge at any magnification and no bias. The
answer on maps is density and filtering: fit a map to each caster so a
texel is no larger than a pixel, and make the edge by filtering then
steepening. **Confirmed: shadow maps.** The "volumes for closed casters"
fallback should go; nothing in the record or the prototype asks for it.

## 4. What was missing

- **A budget.** None in milliseconds, and no rule for what gives way.
- **A light policy.** Which live lights are drawn, how many, how they are
  ranked, and which may cast.
- **The flashlight and the first-person light pass.** Retail redraws up to
  eight lights over the first-person view; the page does not mention it.
- **Weapon, plasma and explosion lights.** They are effect-part and
  attachment lights with short lives; the page names them in the target
  look only.
- **Self-shadowing.** A goal with no mechanism.
- **60 and 120 fps, and split screen.**
- **How a picture is checked.** The page's diagnostics are environment
  variables on the D3D path. Pictures are checked with `render.capture`
  and `render.compare`.
- **Where the hard edge comes from when the map is coarse.** A street
  lamp's cone covers far more screen than a map of any size we would give
  it has texels at 4x.

## 5. What to do

1. Stop work on the D3D-path row. It keeps working until its code is
   deleted with the rest of `scaled.rs`.
2. Rewrite the design on the native renderer over `FrameScene`, with the
   scene the same whatever the mode. Per-caster depth maps for the sun on
   objects, retail's own light passes for more lights, retail's own shadow
   layers at a larger size with a steep filtered edge.
3. Start the two pieces that depend on nothing: the static research the
   design still needs, and the edge mode in the pixel translator. Start the
   scene data after FrameScene slice 3 and the passes after slice 6.
4. Budget 3 ms of GPU time at 4x on the M3 (**Estimate**) and measure it in
   the first slice that draws.

The rewritten page has the design, the budget and the slices.
